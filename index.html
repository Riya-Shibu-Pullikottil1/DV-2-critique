Malaysia's Demographic Transition

How to read this chart

Each grey line connects a state's birth rate in 2000 with its birth rate in 2023. The blue dot shows the earlier rate, while the orange dot shows the later rate. Longer lines represent larger declines.

The chart shows that Malaysia's falling birth rate is not only a national trend. Every state in this comparison recorded fewer births per 1,000 people by 2023.

2023 snapshot

Malaysia still recorded more births than deaths in 2023.

For every 1 death, there were about 2.3 births. This means the population was still naturally increasing, but the margin is becoming smaller.

Key immunisation insights

Immunisation coverage is generally high by 2023, with average reported coverage reaching about 105.9% across the vaccines with available 2023 values.

MMR shows the weakest average coverage among the consistently reported vaccines, while Polio records the strongest average coverage across the period.

The lowest overall immunisation year is 2002, when average coverage across diseases was about 69.8%. Polio coverage is around 22.5 percentage points higher than MMR on average.

Births by ethnicity

Birth Patterns Differ in Absolute and Relative Terms

In absolute terms, bumi_malay records the highest number of births across the period. However, when births are adjusted by population size, other_citizen records the highest average birth rate per 1,000 people. This shows that the group with the largest total number of births is not always the group with the highest birth rate relative to its population size.

Early childhood deaths are not changing equally

The small multiples show that early childhood death patterns differ by category. Toddler death rates remain very low across the period, while infant, neonatal and perinatal categories show more visible movement.

This suggests that Malaysia’s childhood mortality issue is more concentrated around birth and early infancy rather than later childhood.
